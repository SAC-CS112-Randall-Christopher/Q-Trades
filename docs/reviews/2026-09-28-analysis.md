# Q-Trades: current system versus the stronger product brief

**Review date:** September 28, 2026. **Scope:** uploaded `Q-Trades.zip`, not a refreshed GitHub branch or a live workstation audit. **Disposition:** retain the existing system and finish the research-to-forward-evaluation handoff. Do not rebuild it around a larger agent framework.

## Executive conclusion

Q-Trades is already a useful local, paper-only research application. It has real public-data adapters, a deterministic simulator, a durable financial journal, a dashboard, strategy comparisons and a genuinely fitted numerical-model prototype. It is not yet an integrated system that repeatedly discovers, validates and deploys better strategies to isolated prospective paper accounts.

The primary improvement is a trustworthy, complete loop:

**observed market data → registered hypothesis → protected experiment → frozen challenger → forward paper comparison → retain/reject → evidence-linked learning journal.**

This is an engineering/product recommendation, not evidence that an exploitable trading edge has been found. Several parts of this direction already appear in `docs/research/2026-09-27-crypto-learning-plan.md`; that existing plan should be completed rather than recreated under new names.

## What to preserve

- **Financial authority:** `src/trading/paper_store.py:53–131` uses an exclusive writer, row locking, a transaction containing journal/state updates and invariant checks. Append-only database triggers protect financial evidence (`15–41`). These are source observations; database behavior was not reverified against PostgreSQL here.
- **Precise accounting:** Decimal arithmetic, cash reservations, per-asset balancing, fee records, original funding and retained losses already exist in `paper_engine.py`. Do not substitute an LLM-maintained balance or a second ledger.
- **Useful simulation mechanics:** `paper_engine.py:66–88,315–350` uses later observations, displayed-depth participation, rounding, fees and conservative price adjustments, rather than awarding an entry at the closing price that generated the signal.
- **Frozen comparisons:** three existing breakout variants in `paper_strategy.py:11–15` are valuable baselines. Isolated comparison balances are hypothetical; they do not multiply eventual live capital.
- **Honest research:** `research_experiment.py:130–207` really fits a numerical coefficient using a chronological train/test split. The retained numerical preflight explicitly rejected a forward-review claim.
- **User-facing work:** the React interface already exposes paper status, balances, strategy rules, signal conditions, model qualification, operational status and explanatory caveats. This review inspected source, not a running browser session.
- **Resource discipline:** existing local inference/resource qualification and restricted advisory-role contracts should be reused. Do not spend unlimited time qualifying increasingly large local models before completing the numerical experiment workflow.

## Findings, ranked by practical consequence

### F01 — New risk can be added when existing inventory cannot be valued
**Priority:** P1 correctness. **Status:** reproduced against unchanged uploaded code, with disposable synthetic state.

`paper_engine.py:790–819` calls `value()` before processing pending orders but does not use that first call's return value to stop pending buys. `fill():315–328` checks several entry permissions, but not account-wide valuation freshness. A later freshness check stops *new intents*, too late to protect a previously reserved buy.

Reproduction: primary owns BTC; reserve an ETH buy using valid observations; remove the BTC observation while keeping a later fresh ETH book. The next tick fills the ETH buy, resulting in BTC and ETH positions while `valuation_fresh` is false. Cash was reserved, so this is not proof of overspending. It is proof that exposure can increase without a current account valuation.

**Correction:** cancel/reject risk-increasing paper fills when required portfolio valuation is unavailable. Keep risk-reducing exits possible when their own instrument data is adequate. Surface the exact blocked holding and recovery action in the UI. Test valuation loss between reservation and fill, not only before reservation. For any future live adapter, a cancellation request must not be treated as a confirmed cancellation.

### F02 — A scheduled review automatically re-arms a drawdown stop
**Priority:** P1 policy/financial boundary. **Status:** reproduced; partly a legacy policy conflict, not evidence of unauthorized live trading.

`paper_engine.py:302–303` pauses at 35% below its risk peak. `review():724–737` resets the risk peak to current equity, clears the pause and adds only a ten-minute cooldown. Reproduction moved a $100 reference account to $64; the review changed the reference peak to $64 and `drawdown_pause` from true to false. The lifetime drawdown remained 36%: historical loss was not erased.

Repeated resets could allow further losses beyond the original risk segment. Earlier instructions explicitly allowed aggressive paper attempts and a replenishment experiment, so automatic recovery should not be retroactively described as a violation of that historical contract. It is nevertheless unsuitable as an implicit policy for the stronger brief.

**Correction:** introduce an explicit, frozen distinction between a temporary entry pause, a hard drawdown stop and an authorized new research attempt. A scheduled model review may recommend an action; it must not silently grant a fresh loss budget. Preserve historical attempts and their original policy. Do not impose approval on every ordinary trade.

### F03 — Promotion optimizes completed-trade averages, not whole-account results
**Priority:** P1 evaluation integrity. **Status:** source-confirmed.

`paper_engine.py:665–723` selects trades opened and closed entirely within each four-hour window, computes arithmetic mean `pnl / cost`, requires ten closed trades for incumbent and challenger in both windows, and ranks eligible candidates by that mean. It also checks stressed closed-trade P&L and lifetime drawdown; those safeguards should not be ignored.

The main score does not directly include idle cash, marked open P&L or trades crossing window boundaries. It is not an equal-capital account return. For illustration only, a 2% gain on $5 earns $0.10, while a 1% gain on $50 earns $0.50. Mean trade return alone ranks the former more highly, although the latter earns more for the account. This is an arithmetic illustration, not an observed trade.

**Correction:** compare flow-adjusted, whole-account equity over identical observation windows. Include open inventory, executable valuation uncertainty, idle cash, fees and turnover. Present operating costs separately and compare with cash and a relevant simple exposure baseline. Retain trade-level metrics as diagnostics. Do not require a slow strategy to manufacture ten trades every four hours. Predeclare uncertainty/sample rules appropriate to its horizon; do not merely remove standards.

### F04 — Fees are a fixed research assumption, not a current venue/account contract
**Priority:** P1 economic validity. **Status:** source-confirmed; external schedule checked September 28, 2026.

`paper_engine.py:9–15` uses a 0.10% fee per side, 2 basis points of adverse price movement per side and 10% displayed-depth participation. `research_experiment.py:147` names the same fee assumption. Hard-coded does not mean the original stress assumption was illegitimate. It does mean a valid strategy can appear uneconomic, or a different venue can appear cheaper than it is, if assumptions are not made explicit and versioned.

The Binance.US public schedule retrieved for this review advertises 0% maker and 0.02% taker on most pairs, with a separate BNB/USD tier. That is public schedule evidence, **not** verification of Chris's eligibility, actual commissions, account terms or funding finality. Binance.US remains a candidate, not an approved venue.

Approximate round-trip fees on equal buy/sell notionals, before spread and slippage:

| Modeled per-side fee | $50 traded each way | $100 traded each way |
|---|---:|---:|
| Existing 0.10% assumption | $0.10 | $0.20 |
| Published 0.02% taker scenario | $0.02 | $0.04 |

These are illustrative calculations, not an instruction to turn over the whole account. Actual sale notional and cash affordability vary. Repeated turnover makes even small fee differences relevant. Neither scenario proves positive returns.

**Correction:** retain the old version as a stress scenario. Add source-dated venue/pair/account fee profiles, minimum notional and precision, fee asset, latency and fill policy. Replay identical candidates under identical profiles. Never rewrite original fees or attribute a fee-model change to better strategy skill. Do not award maker fees to marketable orders or assume a touched resting price proves a fill. Do not double-charge spread/slippage already embedded in executable fills.

### F05 — Holdout protection depends on the caller, not an enforced research service
**Priority:** P1 before autonomous search. **Status:** reproduced; no claim that the documented original preflight leaked.

`research_experiment.py:130–159` accepts `prior_test_end=0` by default. Running a second feature on the same data without carrying forward the consumed boundary completes. Supplying the boundary correctly returns insufficient new data. The original preflight documentation already warns about this.

The source tree contains the numerical function and its tests, but no production invocation of `run_experiment` in the scripts/runtime/API. This is an incomplete handoff, not an existing autonomous tuning loop proven to be violating its contract.

**Correction:** make a durable registry own dataset identity, information-availability cutoffs, predeclared comparisons, consumed evaluation windows, models, outcomes and failure records. Import the already consumed boundary **1790595239.9180105**. A changed feature or a new dataset hash must not make overlapping previously inspected time periods unseen again. A predeclared challenger group may be compared on a common final window, but once results influence development, that window is no longer untouched evidence. Previously seen data may later become training data without being relabeled as a fresh final test.

### F06 — Real numerical learning exists, but is not connected to forward accounts
**Priority:** P2 next product capability. **Status:** source-confirmed and retained result reproduced.

Existing primary adaptation selects `breakout-v1`, `responsive-v1` or `selective-v1`. These are different parameters within one family, not economically independent strategy families. Separately, `quote-ridge-v1` fits a model to historical observations. Model-role qualification checks advisory behavior; it does not establish trading performance.

The retained BTCUSD preflight contains **674 quote rows**, **634 matured examples**, **431 training examples** and **191 test examples**. Forecast MSE was **105.14125772** versus a training-mean baseline of **108.73830186**, a **3.307983%** reduction. There were **zero positive-after-cost predictions** and `eligible_for_forward_review=false`. Reproducing these values is not new independent market evidence. Overlapping labels and a short observation period limit inference.

**Correction:** connect one registered hypothesis to one deterministic numerical experiment and its resulting retained artifact; then expose an eligible frozen artifact to a separate prospective paper strategy through the existing risk/ledger engine. A numerical prediction is not an order. Add a rejection report when the model cannot justify acting. The first useful successful workflow may end with a well-supported 'do not promote.'

### F07 — Current policy documents and the new brief need a deliberate reconciliation
**Priority:** P1 before activation of a new profile. **Status:** document comparison.

The uploaded `AGENTS.md` includes historical permissions for options paper research, a $100-to-$1,000 attempt objective and fake replenishment after failure. `docs/NEXT_CHECKPOINT.md` still gives substantial options follow-up work. The stronger brief excludes derivatives, rejects automatic top-ups and makes full-account growth the primary goal.

**Correction:** new default work should be paper-only, cash-funded crypto spot first, no derivatives exposure, no automatic replenishment and no hidden loss-budget resets. Archive—not erase—old options and replenishment evidence. Keep any legacy attempt as a clearly labeled historical/frozen experiment; do not stop or migrate a live local process without inspecting it and protecting its journal. Observing a public derivatives market as research data is not the same as holding derivative exposure; treat any such feature as unproven and do not let it expand permissions. Preserve all three long-term tier objectives, but do not pretend the current three breakout variants implement all three tiers.

Private GitHub source storage is authorized by the current request. It does **not** authorize public hosting, a shared True to Plan database, a remote live account, paid inference, deployment or a merge.

### F08 — Product UI should explain research outcomes, not primarily model activity
**Priority:** P2, included in each vertical checkpoint. **Status:** source review; no browser acceptance claim.

`apps/web/src/StrategyLab.tsx` explains fixed breakout comparisons and their current limits. `main.tsx` includes paper, options, model trials and operations views. These are useful surfaces, but they do not yet provide one complete numerical-hypothesis-to-forward-result journey.

**Correction:** make the landing view answer: What is being tested? Has net account performance improved? Against what? What blocked an action? What changed? What remains uncertain? What is the next action? Keep technical qualification transcripts behind diagnostics. Show research/replay/forward-paper/live states and strategy/execution versions unmistakably. Include a causal receipt assembled from recorded signal/cost/risk data; generated explanations may summarize but must not invent a reason after the fact.

## The smallest credible architecture

Keep one application/repository with the existing web client, API, market observer and exclusive deterministic financial writer. Keep the existing research worker and restricted advisory interfaces. Extend the research/tool journal instead of creating another financial authority. Add an immutable dataset/model artifact store and durable experiment metadata only where they protect reproducibility and evaluation boundaries.

The research side proposes and evaluates. The numerical strategy supplies a bounded signal. The financial engine remains independently responsible for reservations, exposure, fills and accounting. The UI reads both through defined interfaces. No LLM is required in the quote or order path, and position management must not depend on its availability.

A private repo makes code available from other machines. It does not provide a synchronized running database or a safe multi-writer trading service. Each research environment needs its own local configuration; financial journals require a separate backup/restore design.

|---|---|---|
| A — Reliable paper boundaries | Understand why new risk stopped and recover deliberately | Stale held-asset valuation cannot permit a pending buy; exits remain possible; scheduled reviews cannot silently reset a hard stop; UI and restart behavior agree |

All work remains paper-only. Live readiness is a later evidence packet, not an automatically enabled next state. Do not delay A–C for a broad broker survey or a new agent framework.

### First implementation task: checkpoint A

**Goal:** no risk-increasing paper fill can bypass a stale portfolio valuation or a latched loss stop, and the operator can understand/recover through the normal UI.

**Inspect first:** current branch/status, all applicable instructions, `paper_engine.py`, `paper_store.py`, `paper_runtime.py`, `tiered_runtime.py`, the paper control API and `PaperPanel.tsx`/`StrategyLab.tsx`. Refresh real runtime state before touching a running experiment.

**Implementation boundary:** reuse the existing engine and financial journal; make entry-vs-exit policy explicit; retain old-policy evidence. Introduce the smallest versioned risk-policy change necessary. Do not alter strategies, fees, model qualification profiles, options history or funding totals in this checkpoint.

**Required checks:** missing BTC value with pending ETH buy; stale value between reservation and fill; recovery with no duplicate fill; risk-reducing exit while another holding is stale; hard drawdown stop across scheduled review and restart; repeated resume request is idempotent; operator pause still honored; transaction rollback retains prior state; journal balances; UI shows specific cause and normal recovery path. Use disposable databases for storage tests. Include both expected success and rejected-action paths.

**Non-goals:** live adapter, new strategy families, fee recalibration, fresh funding, model deployment, hosting, broad schema replacement or a production service restart.

## Initial research priority, not claimed strategy profitability

1. Keep the existing breakout family as a reproducible baseline; add cash and a simple exposure benchmark. Test whether confirmation, spread and holding-period choices survive current/stressed execution costs.
2. Compare a slower trend/momentum challenger to reduce dependence on short-horizon timing. Reject it if apparent benefit disappears against a matching exposure baseline.
3. Test regime-conditioned mean reversion as a distinct mechanism, with explicit trend-failure conditions and identical evaluation discipline.
4. Investigate relative-strength rotation only with a fixed point-in-time universe and delisting/membership handling.
5. Defer order-flow, maker-inventory and arbitrage claims until real stream completeness, timing, queue/non-fill behavior and funded legs can be measured. Defer reinforcement learning until the simulator cannot be rewarded for exploiting its own inaccuracies.

This ranking is an engineering order based on present capabilities and data needs, not a forecast of returns. Preserve Tier 1 longer-horizon diversified research as a separate later lane; do not represent speculative crypto exposure as its implementation.
